Problem Statement

The Core Problem

Americans don't explain how their pricing system works because they can't imagine not knowing it.

International visitors to the 2026 World Cup arrive with expectations shaped by their home countries' systems:

  • VAT refunds (Europe, Australia, Canada)
  • Comprehensive public transit (Europe, Asia)
  • Included pricing (VAT systems show final prices)
  • Universal healthcare coverage
  • Minimal/optional tipping (5-10% vs 18-22% mandatory)
  • Different legal frameworks (cannabis, firearms, alcohol)

The American Reality

A pricing system designed to show lower advertised costs while extracting maximum dollars through hidden fees, mandatory tipping, and taxes added at checkout.

Specific Pain Points

Financial Surprises

  • That "$200/night" Boston hotel? Actually $701 after resort fee ($40), tax (14.75% = $87), and parking ($60)
  • Five nights in Boston = $3,685 (Americans call this "normal." Visitors call it theft.)
  • Menu "$20 burger" + sales tax (6.25%) + mandatory tip (20%) = $25.25 actual cost
  • 14 days × 3 meals = $529 hidden on top of menu prices
  • Medical insurance required ($200-400) - ER visits cost $1,000-3,000+

Legal Traps

  • Cannabis: Legal in CA/WA/NY/MA, FELONY in TX/GA (180 days jail for <2oz)
  • Firearms: Open carry legal in TX/MO, banned in CA/FL/NY
  • Federal property: Cannabis always illegal (parks, federal buildings)

Match Day Surge Pricing

Kansas City normally $121/night. Argentina match day? $654/night—a 440% increase

Boston hotels: $700+/night during World Cup. But stay in Providence (22 miles closer to "Boston's" stadium)? $400/night. Geographic arbitrage saves $1,500 for 5 nights.

Why It Fits: International visitors NEED this (US healthcare costs $1,000-3,000+ for ER visits). Already recommended in guide as genuine value-add.

Quality Standards

Partner Selection Criteria:

  1. Genuine utility - Does this actually help international visitors?
  2. Pricing transparency - Do they show real costs upfront?
  3. International-friendly - Do they work for non-US customers?
  4. Brand reputation - Are they established and trustworthy?
  5. Fair commission - Is the payout worth the recommendation?

What We Won't Promote:

  • ❌ Products/services we haven't personally vetted
  • ❌ High-pressure upsells or scammy offers
  • ❌ Anything conflicting with "financial self-defense" positioning
  • ❌ Partners that hide fees or use deceptive pricing
